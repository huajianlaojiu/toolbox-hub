About the Audio Converter

The Audio Converter changes audio files between WAV, MP3, M4A and OGG entirely in your browser, using the Web Audio API for decoding and encoding. No file is uploaded, which matters when the audio is unreleased music or a private recording.

WAV and MP3 cover almost every need: WAV is the lossless intermediate editors expect, MP3 is the universal share format. M4A and OGG are rendered through MediaRecorder for browsers that support them. Sample rate conversion (44.1 kHz, 48 kHz, 22.05 kHz, 16 kHz) is built in, so you can prepare audio for video work, voice notes or telephony-grade files.

Paste a track, pick a format, download. That is the whole tool.

Where people use the Audio Converter

  • Turning a WAV master into a shareable MP3.
  • Converting a voice memo to the format a platform expects.
  • Normalising sample rate before dropping audio into a video edit.

Tips and best practices

  • Convert to WAV when you will edit further; MP3 last.
  • For voice, 22.05 kHz mono MP3 at 96 kbps is tiny and clear.

Frequently asked questions

Which formats can I convert from?

Anything your browser can decode, which in practice means MP3, WAV, OGG, M4A/AAC, FLAC and Opus.

Is MP3 encoding really local?

Yes. The MP3 encoder (lamejs) runs as WebAssembly inside the page. The only download is the encoder library itself on first use.

Why is M4A or OGG sometimes unavailable?

Those formats depend on MediaRecorder support, which varies by browser. WAV and MP3 always work.

Does sample rate conversion lose quality?

Converting to a lower rate discards data by definition; converting upward does not add detail. Match the rate to the destination platform.
