Shortcuts: Space = play/pause · ↑/↓ = speed · R = restart

Online teleprompter with auto scroll All processing happens in your browser.

About the Teleprompter

This online teleprompter turns any device with a browser into a prompting screen. Paste a script or import a .txt or .srt file, and the text auto-scrolls at a speed you set. Adjust font size and line height for your reading distance, mirror the text for beam-splitter rigs, and go fullscreen so the script fills the screen while you record.

Keyboard shortcuts make it usable mid-take: Space plays or pauses, arrow keys change speed, R restarts. Your script is remembered in this browser, so reopening the page brings it back.

Everything runs locally. No script is uploaded, no account is needed, and it works offline once the page has loaded.

Where people use the Teleprompter

  • Recording talking-head video without memorising lines.
  • Reading a long script on a second screen while filming.
  • Presenting to a camera with steady eye line.

How to use the Teleprompter

  1. 1

    Add your script

    Paste text or import a .txt / .srt file. Subtitle files are stripped to plain text automatically.

  2. 2

    Set your comfort

    Adjust speed, font size and line height. The reading line marks your focal point.

  3. 3

    Present

    Press Space or Play. Mirror if your rig needs it, fullscreen for a clean screen.

  4. 4

    Reuse

    Your script stays in this browser; reopen the page to continue.

Tips and best practices

  • Start slower than feels natural; speed up once you find your rhythm.
  • Short lines read better than long paragraphs on a prompter.

Frequently asked questions

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. Any modern browser works, and a phone or tablet makes an excellent second-screen prompter.

Can I use it with a physical teleprompter rig?

Yes. Mirror mode flips the text horizontally for beam-splitter setups; keep your device in landscape for the widest lines.

Is my script uploaded?

No. It is stored only in this browser's local storage and never sent anywhere.

Does it support SRT files?

Yes. Import a .srt or .vtt and the timecodes are stripped, leaving clean narration text.
