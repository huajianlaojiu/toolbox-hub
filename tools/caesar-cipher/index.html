About the Caesar Cipher

The Caesar Cipher shifts every letter in a message by a fixed number of places through the alphabet, wrapping around at Z back to A. It is the oldest documented cipher and still the clearest way to understand what substitution means, because the whole system is one number. Here you supply both the text and the shift: type your message, then a vertical bar, then the shift, for example hello|3. If you omit the shift, 3 is used, which is the value Caesar himself is said to have used. Treat it as a teaching tool and a puzzle device only; it is trivially breakable.

Where people use the Caesar Cipher

  • Explaining how substitution ciphers work to someone learning.
  • Solving or setting a puzzle or a geocache clue.
  • Hiding a spoiler in plain sight from someone not paying attention.
  • Checking a manual ROT13 or ROT-n result.

How to use the Caesar Cipher

  1. 1

    Type your message and the shift

    Use the form text|shift, for example hello|3. Anything after the bar is read as the shift, and if it is missing or unreadable the default of 3 is applied.

  2. 2

    Run it

    The output shows the shift that was used and the transformed text.

  3. 3

    Decode by using the reverse shift

    To undo a shift of 3, apply a shift of 23, because 3 plus 23 is 26 and the alphabet wraps. Equivalently, apply the same shift another 25 times.

  4. 4

    Try every shift if you do not know it

    There are only 25 meaningful shifts. Running all of them and reading the one that looks like language is the whole of breaking this cipher.

Tips and best practices

  • Remember that a shift of 13 is its own inverse, which is why ROT13 is used for spoilers.
  • To reverse any shift n, apply 26 minus n.
  • Leave spaces and punctuation in place; they are what makes the result readable as a puzzle.

Frequently asked questions

Why is 3 the default?

Because a shift of 3 is the one attributed to Julius Caesar in surviving accounts. It is a convention, not a mathematical requirement; any value from 1 to 25 works.

How do I decrypt without knowing the shift?

Try all 25 shifts and read the results. That takes seconds by hand and is why this cipher offers no real security. Frequency analysis, looking at which letters appear most often, is the systematic version of the same idea.

Are spaces and punctuation encrypted?

No. Only letters are shifted; digits, spaces and punctuation pass through unchanged. That is one reason the result is so easy to read once you know the method.

Does case survive?

Yes. Uppercase letters are shifted within the uppercase alphabet and lowercase within the lowercase one, so capitalisation is preserved.

Is this secure enough to send a real secret?

No, not remotely. It has 25 possible keys and no protection against frequency analysis. It is fine for puzzles, teaching and hiding spoilers from a casual glance.
